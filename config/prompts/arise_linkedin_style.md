# ARISE LinkedIn Draft Style Guide

This document defines the *style* ARISE-style LinkedIn drafts should follow.
The reference exemplar lives at
[`arise_linkedin_example.md`](./arise_linkedin_example.md).

## How to use this file

This is a **style reference, not a factual template.**

- Never copy the example's facts, numbers, model names, statistics, or
  claims into a draft for a different paper.
- Every factual detail in a generated draft — the journal, the finding, the
  method, the examples, the statistics, the takeaway — must come from the
  new paper's own source material (abstract, metadata, or full text),
  never from the exemplar.
- If the new paper doesn't supply enough detail to fill a section below
  (e.g. no concrete number or example is available), leave that section
  thin or omit it rather than inventing or borrowing one from the
  exemplar.
- The exemplar is a single sample from one domain (clinical AI
  benchmarking). Its specific structure should flex to fit whatever the
  new paper is actually about.

## Style principles

1. **Engaging but professional opening.** Lead with a hook, not a dry
   restatement of the title. A relevant emoji or two is fine; keep the
   tone credible, not sensational.
2. **Mention the journal and the ARISE affiliate.** Name the publication
   venue and the ARISE-affiliated author(s) or team early, so the post is
   clearly anchored to ARISE and to a real, citable source.
3. **Explain the central finding in plain language.** State the paper's
   main point in a sentence or two a non-specialist could follow — no
   jargon dump.
4. **Describe what the researchers did.** Give a brief, concrete sense of
   the method or study design (what they built, tested, measured, or
   compared) so the finding reads as earned, not asserted.
5. **Use one or two memorable, source-supported examples or statistics.**
   Pick the most illustrative concrete detail(s) actually present in the
   paper's source material — a striking number, a specific test case, a
   comparison. Do not manufacture an example or reuse the exemplar's.
6. **Explain the surprising result or paradox.** If the paper has a
   counterintuitive or noteworthy twist, name it explicitly and explain
   why it's surprising — this is often the most shareable part of the
   post.
7. **End with a practical takeaway.** Close the narrative with what this
   means for someone actually building, evaluating, or deploying clinical
   AI — actionable, not just descriptive.
8. **Invite readers to read the paper or share thoughts.** A short,
   direct call to action (e.g. a question to readers, a pointer to the
   paper link below the post).
9. **Use a concise set of relevant LinkedIn hashtags.** A handful of
   topical hashtags drawn from the paper's actual subject matter (e.g.
   its field, method, or application area) — not a copy of the exemplar's
   hashtag list unless they genuinely apply.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Treat the example as a style reference, not a factual template.
- Never copy its facts, numbers, models, or claims into another paper's
  post.
- Every generated detail must come from the new paper's source material.
- All generated drafts remain human-reviewed drafts — this style guide
  does not authorize autonomous posting.
